Concept · Step 01

Renting vs Buying Computers

Traditionally, running software meant buying physical servers — expensive hardware you own, maintain, and pay for whether you use it or not. Cloud computing flips this: you rent computing power instead, and pay only for what you use.

🖥️ Buying Your Own Servers

$$$$ hardware upfront ↓ You handle repairs & security ↓ Pay even while idle

Real-World Application

This is why almost every startup and most large companies today run on cloud infrastructure instead of owning server rooms — it removes the massive upfront cost and lets a small team manage infrastructure that once needed an entire IT department.

Concept · Step 02

What AWS Is

AWS (Amazon Web Services) is Amazon's cloud computing platform — one of the largest providers of rented computing infrastructure in the world. It offers hundreds of services — storage, servers, databases, AI tools — that you rent and combine as needed.

Concept · Step 04 · 🔔 Safety-Critical

Why The Budget Alert Matters

AWS Free Tier has usage limits — going over them can result in real charges, even accidentally. A budget alert emails you automatically the moment spending crosses your chosen threshold — it does not stop charges by itself, but it warns you early enough to act.

🔔
Set It Once, Forget The Worry

A $1 budget alert means you'll know the moment something unexpected starts costing money — before it becomes a real bill. This is a simple, one-time setup that protects you for the rest of the course.

💡
Real-World Application

Professionals set budget alerts on every cloud account they touch — it's a five-minute habit that has saved countless developers from an unpleasant, unexpected bill.
